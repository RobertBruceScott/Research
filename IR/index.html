At low doses, ionizing radiation is often considered an acceptible minimal risk, although no universally risk-free threshold has been established for long-term effects such as cancer. Radiation-protection standards are based largely on population-average risks, whereas certain inherited variants—particularly those affecting DNA-damage recognition and repair—can make some individuals unusually sensitive to radiation or more susceptible to radiation-induced cancer. DNA damage is also an important contributor to normal ageing and interacts bidirectionally with the epigenetic alterations that accumulate over time.
